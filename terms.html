What Calmer is

Calmer is a self-help tool for anxiety. It uses methods from cognitive behavioural therapy and related approaches to help you notice patterns, get through difficult moments and practise skills. It is not a medical device, a diagnosis, a treatment, a therapist or a crisis service, and it does not give medical advice.

The lessons, programmes and tools are general. They are not tailored to your health, your history or any medication you take.

Your health

If anxiety is affecting your daily life, please talk to your GP. In England you can also refer yourself to NHS Talking Therapies, free. Do not use Calmer in place of professional care or against medical advice, and do not stop or change any medication because of anything in the app.

Some exercises, such as breath holds or facing a fear, can feel uncomfortable. Go at your own pace and stop if you feel faint, dizzy or unwell. If you have a heart or breathing condition, are pregnant, or have had panic attacks with chest pain, check with a doctor before using breath-hold patterns, and get chest pain checked rather than assuming it is anxiety.

In a crisis

Calmer cannot respond to an emergency and nobody monitors what you write. If you or someone else is in danger, call 999. Samaritans are free on 116 123, any time. You can text SHOUT to 85258. In England, NHS 111 option 2 is for urgent mental health help.

The crisis card and the support card are there to help you reach people. They may not appear every time they could help, and they may sometimes appear when they are not needed.

Insights, questionnaires and reports

Insights show patterns in what you have recorded. A pattern is not proof that one thing causes another, and an early sign may change as you add more. The GAD-7 is a screening questionnaire, not a diagnosis. Reports are summaries of what you entered, to support a conversation with a professional, not clinical records.

Reminders and widgets

Reminders are gentle and may arrive a few minutes late, or not at all if Android holds them back. Do not rely on Calmer for anything time-critical, such as taking medication.
